Tuition LMS

A web-based learning platform for a tuition centre: students browse and enrol in courses, open materials (PDFs, videos, quizzes) and track their progress, while parents see attendance, marks and fees, and the centre administrator sees enrolments, class capacity and revenue. It exists first as a client demo — something Luqman can show a prospective client on a gig platform — and second as the base for a real system that can be sold more than once.

Why this project exists

A tuition centre listed a job on a Malaysian gig platform for a custom web platform, budget RM3,300. The listing gates on "must have previous projects like this done", which Luqman does not yet have. The honest way past that gate is to build the thing for real rather than claim past work — and the same codebase then serves every future tuition-centre client, of which the platform shows a steady supply.

The underlying problem for a tuition centre is that course materials live in WhatsApp groups and Google Drive folders, attendance is on paper, and parents have no visibility into whether their child is actually keeping up until the trial exam results arrive.

What counts as "done"

For the demo (phase 0): a link Luqman can send from his phone that a non-technical centre owner can click through and recognise as their own business — their subjects, their fee structure, their three audiences.

For a real build: a centre can add a class, upload materials to it, enrol a student, and have that student's parent see attendance and marks — all without Luqman touching the database.

What is NOT in scope

  • Online fee payment (FPX / Billplz / ToyyibPay) — priced separately if a client wants it
  • Video hosting and streaming costs — the client's account, not Luqman's
  • Mobile apps; this is a responsive web platform only
  • Timetabling or teacher payroll

01-idea.md · 7 sections

The idea

Problem

A small tuition centre runs on WhatsApp groups, printed worksheets and a paper attendance book. Students lose track of which notes belong to which chapter, parents cannot tell whether their child attended last Tuesday or how they scored, and the centre owner cannot see at a glance which classes are full, who has not paid, or which students are falling behind. Nobody in the building has time to fix this, and off-the-shelf systems like Google Classroom are built for schools, not for a fee-charging centre that needs enrolment and billing visibility.

Proposed solution

A single web platform with three doors into the same data. Students browse the term's classes, enrol, work through the materials for each class and see their own completion percentage. Parents see one child's attendance, marks and outstanding fees. The administrator sees every student, every class's remaining seats, monthly revenue and who needs chasing. Materials are PDFs, recorded class videos and auto-marked practice quizzes.

Target users

SegmentNeedWhy they'd pick us
Tuition centre ownerSee enrolment, capacity and unpaid fees without a spreadsheetBuilt around fees and enrolment, which school LMSs ignore
Student (Form 4–5 / SPM)Find the right notes and know what to do nextMaterials organised by class and chapter, with a visible next step
ParentKnow their child is attending and improving, before the trial resultsAttendance and marks visible the same day, not at the end of term

Value / advantage

  • Luqman works at a vocational college, so he knows what course delivery, enrolment and progress tracking actually look like from the inside. That domain knowledge is scarcer among gig-platform freelancers than the coding is.
  • Once built, the codebase is resellable. Tuition-centre platform listings recur, so the second client costs a fraction of the first.

06-decisions.md · 5 sections

Decisions

2026-09-10 — Build a labelled prototype rather than claim prior work

The listing requires "previous projects like this done" and Luqman has none. The options were to overstate his history, to skip the job, or to build something real he can honestly show. We built a clickable prototype and labelled it a prototype with sample data in a bar across the top of every screen, so nothing about it presents itself as a delivered client system. The demo answers the client's real question — can this person build it — without answering it falsely.

2026-09-10 — Single-file vanilla HTML for the demo, not the real stack

The demo needed to open in one tap from a chat message on a client's phone. A Next.js app would have meant accounts, a build step and a deploy before anything could be shown. Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS in one file, published as an Artifact, gives a URL immediately and costs nothing to host. The real build is not bound by this choice; the demo is a sales tool, not a foundation.

2026-09-10 — Sample data is Malaysian and specific

The courses are Additional Mathematics Form 4, Physics and Chemistry Form 5, and Bahasa Melayu, Sejarah and English at SPM level, taught by named teachers at real Malaysian fee levels (RM150–180 a month). A tuition centre owner recognises their own business immediately, which a demo full of "Course 1 / Course 2" never achieves. Interface text stays in English per the standing convention; the subject names, teacher honorific and the Bahasa Melayu quiz content are the subject's own vocabulary, not interface copy.

2026-09-10 — Hosting accounts belong to the client

Supabase's free tier gives 1GB of file storage, which one course video exhausts. Pro is about USD 25 a month, plus Vercel — call it RM120–210 a month, or RM1,400–2,500 a year against a RM3,300 fee. Any real build registers those accounts in the client's name on the client's card from day one, rather than on Luqman's with a promise to bill it back.

2026-09-10 — Private repo, proprietary licence

The plan for this codebase is to sell it to more than one tuition centre, since the platform's economics only work if the second client costs a fraction of the first. A public MIT repository would let anyone bidding against Luqman clone the whole product and undercut him with it. The repository is private and the licence line was changed from MIT to proprietary before the first push. Client engagements buy a licence to their own deployment, not to the source.

This is the opposite call to print-quote, deliberately: that one's value is that people can read it, this one's value is that they cannot.
