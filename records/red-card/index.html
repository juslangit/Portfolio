Red CardProject record · built 2026-09-18

Red Card

A first-person football refereeing game for Windows and Mac. You are the referee, on foot, on the pitch, for a full 11-a-side match that the game simulates for real. You run, sprint, turn your head and look down at your own body. The game knows the truth of every tackle, every touch and every ball over the line; your job is to see it and get it right. An assessor marks you out of ten afterwards, and a good career climbs from a village field to a cup final with VAR in your ear.

How the game gets made

Pipeline

Every feature has gone round the same loop: decide what the game must and must not do, look up how the real sport does it, build it, prove it with a headless check, look at it, and only then merge it. The notes further down are the record of each pass.

  1. IdeaWhat is the game, and what must it never do? One round of questions to Luqman before building.01-idea.md
  2. LawsEvery decision is checked against the IFAB Laws of the Game - Law 1 for the field, 9 for out of play, 11 for offside, 12 for fouls and cards.pitch_spec.gd, laws.gd
  3. BuildGDScript in Godot 4.7.2; football clips in Blender on the Meshy rig; sound through sfx.scripts/, tools/
  4. CheckHeadless scenes: whole bot-refereed matches, and every drill played right (must pass) and ignored (must fail).dev/checks/
  5. LookScreenshots at exact moments, read back and judged by eye.dev/looks/, dev/shots/
  6. BranchOne branch per piece of work; main at Luqman's word.git
  7. ExportmacOS universal and a single-file Windows .exe.export_presets.cfg, build/

01-idea.md · 5 sections

Idea

The brief (Luqman, 2026-09-18)

"The game is player in first person view will be a referee for football. The game will consist only one sport. Use resources that you already have from Referee For Fun or any other to make this game. Player can move, walk, run, head movement to look at body and legs. The game is for Windows and Mac."

Proposed solution

A physical match in which 22 AI players really play: the ball has physics, tackles have outcomes, and every foul, handball, offside and ball out of play is recorded as a fact before the referee reacts. The referee is a full-body character in first person who must stay close to play and at a good angle to see it. Decisions are physical gestures — whistle, point, show a card to the player you are looking at — and are compared to the truth. The assessor's report is the reckoning.

What it borrows from Referee For Fun

  • "The truth is recorded before the player speaks" — the core pattern.
  • The Meshy characters and their clips, the broadcast UI theme, settings, rebindable controls with gamepad support, the audio library and the docs builder.

Side ideas (parking lot)

  • Referee communication kit: hearing the assistants' voices in the headset.
  • Fitness test between seasons (real referees run one).
  • Women's football kits and a women's cup.

02-planning.md · 3 sections

Planning

Scope — v1 (everything Luqman chose on 2026-09-18)

The referee

  • First person, full visible body; walk, jog, sprint, backpedal, strafe; head look independent enough to look down at body and legs.
  • Stamina that drains on sprints and recovers at a walk; late-match fatigue.
  • Head bob; players and the ball can collide with the referee (Law 9 applies).
  • Wrist watch for the match clock and added time; notebook of cards and goals.

The match

  • 11 v 11 on a full pitch with Law 1 markings, short halves.
  • Ball physics; AI that passes, dribbles, shoots, tackles, keeps goal, takes restarts.
  • Truth recorded for: fouls (careless / reckless / excessive force), holding, handball, simulation, dissent, offside, ball out of play and who touched it last, goals, the ball hitting the referee.

The referee's decisions

Whistle; direct and indirect free kick; penalty; advantage (and coming back to it); yellow, second yellow, red; throw-in, corner, goal kick; offside with two assistants who flag (and can be wrong); goal / no goal; dropped ball; kick-off; start and end of each half; substitutions; added time.

Around the matches

  • Career: village field → town ground → league stadium → cup final (with VAR).
  • Quick match, training drills, scenario challenges.
  • Live reactions: protests, benches, crowd.
  • Assessor's report with marks and replays of key decisions; VAR reviews at the top.

06-decisions.md · 10 sections

Decisions

D-001 — Honest referee, not a corrupt one (2026-09-18)

Luqman chose the honest sim over copying Referee For Fun's bend-the-calls hook. That keeps the two games distinct: Referee For Fun is about getting away with it, Red Card is about getting it right.

D-002 — Godot 4.7 in a new repo, not a fork (2026-09-18)

Same engine as Referee For Fun so the characters, UI theme, settings, controls, audio and docs builder carry over, and it exports to both Windows and macOS. A new repo rather than a fork, because almost none of the net-sport match code applies to football, and a fork would carry thirty thousand lines of it. Unreal was offered and declined: nothing would carry over and the Mac build is heavy.

D-003 — One footballer model, repainted by a shader (2026-09-18)

The Meshy player_red model already wears a football-style kit (shirt with trim, shorts, boots). Every team is that model with the shirt repainted at run time by a shader that finds "the shirt" by its colour, as Referee For Fun's recolour_kit.py does offline. One mesh and one texture for 22 players plus the referee, and any kit colour a team wants without a new texture per team.

D-004 — The referee's body is a footballer's body in referee kit (2026-09-18)

The Meshy official wears trousers — a badminton umpire. A football referee wears a shirt, shorts and socks, so the first-person body is the same player model with the shirt painted referee black (or yellow when a team wears black). The head bone is shrunk to nothing so the camera does not see the inside of its own face.

D-005 — Decisions are made with the arm, not a menu (2026-09-18)

A referee's whole vocabulary is a whistle and an arm, so the restart is given by pointing: right click points where you are looking, and what that means depends on the moment — the way a team attacks for a free kick or throw-in, the corner flag or the goal area after the ball crosses the goal line, the penalty spot, the centre circle for a goal. The line under the crosshair always says what the point would give, so it is never a guess about the controls, only about the football. A radial menu was the alternative; it would have turned the referee's most physical act into a list.

D-006 — Short halves play at three to four times real tempo (2026-09-18)

Luqman chose 11 v 11 with short halves. Real football at real rates squeezed into 12 minutes gave the referee one shot and one ball out a match. The AI shoots, passes and tackles more readily than real players so a match still has around forty decisions in it.

D-007 — Club linesmen at the bottom rung (2026-09-18)

At grassroots level the assistants are volunteers from each club: they flag the ball out, not offside, and they are not neutral. So the first ground teaches the referee to judge offside alone, and neutral assistants arrive with promotion — which is how the real ladder works and gives the career something to change besides the crowd.

D-008 — Simplified spectators at the big grounds (2026-09-18)

Twelve thousand full-detail spectators were 32 million triangles a frame and 10 fps on an M2. Each is now simplified to about 260 triangles with Godot's own mesh simplifier at the two big grounds; the village and town grounds keep full detail, where people are few and close.

D-009 — A single-file Windows build (2026-09-18)

The game data is embedded in the .exe (embed_pck), so the Windows download is one file. Referee For Fun's is still two files that have to travel together, which has been a standing open item there.

D-010 — The whistle-every-restart rule in drills (2026-09-18)

In training and challenges, every restart waits for the whistle, so there is always time to show a card before play goes on. In a match only a penalty, a restart after a card and a kick-off need it, as in the Laws.

Methods

Running it
G=/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot
P=~/Desktop/project/game/red-card
$G --path $P                                    # play
$G --headless --path $P --import                # after adding a class_name
tools/check_scripts.sh                          # parse every script, print only errors
Checks and looks
# A whole match refereed by a bot that reads the truth. Args: ground, seconds per half, speed.
$G --headless --path $P res://dev/checks/_match.tscn -- town 180 10
$G --headless --path $P res://dev/checks/_match.tscn -- town 90 8 verbose   # with a trace

# Every drill and challenge, right and idle. Give ids to run only those, with a trace.
$G --headless --path $P res://dev/checks/_scenarios.tscn
$G --headless --path $P res://dev/checks/_scenarios.tscn -- s_handball

# Screenshots into dev/shots/
$G --path $P --resolution 1600x900 res://dev/looks/_fp.tscn -- town     # first person (also prints fps)
$G --path $P --resolution 1280x720 res://dev/looks/_venue.tscn -- village
$G --path $P --resolution 1280x720 res://dev/looks/_watch.tscn -- town 8
$G --path $P --resolution 1600x900 res://dev/looks/_menu.tscn
Clips
blender --background --python tools/meshy/rig_clips.py -- footballer preview football  # look first
blender --background --python tools/meshy/rig_clips.py -- footballer                   # then forge

The intermediates (footballer_rigged/walking/running/smash.glb) are gitignored copies of Referee For Fun's assets/meshy/player_red/.

Builds
$G --headless --path $P --export-release "macOS" "build/macos/Red Card.zip"
$G --headless --path $P --export-release "Windows" "build/windows/Red Card.exe"

Windows embeds the pack in the exe (binary_format/embed_pck=true), so it is one file — Referee For Fun's Windows build is still two that must travel together.

Patterns established

The truth is recorded before the referee reacts. Laws writes an Incident for every foul, handball, dive, offside, ball out, goal, back-pass, ball-hits-referee, injury and dissent, with the right answer attached. Nothing drawn during play reads it; the assessor's report is the only place it is shown. Carried over from Referee For Fun.

The AI does not know the referee is being tested. Fouls come from tackles rolled from skill, temper and angle (from behind is far likelier to be a foul); the severity is decided there. Players obey the referee even when he is wrong.

Short halves need a match's worth of incidents. Real football squeezed into 12 minutes gives the referee almost nothing to judge, so the AI plays at roughly three to four times real tempo: quicker decisions, readier shooting, sloppier passing, more tackles.

Where the ball goes is measured, not guessed. MatchAI.stats counts passes, shots, tackles and fouls and keeps a histogram of where the ball spends its time along and across the pitch. That is how the AI was found funnelling every attack down the middle (the ball reached a touchline once a match) and fixed with a width bonus in pass choice.

Pressers jockey, they do not stand on toes. A presser 1.1 m from the carrier made every touch a panic and kept the ball in midfield; they now stand off at 1.6–2.8 m by temper.

Keepers decide once, at reaction time. The save first waited until the ball was within 2 m of the keeper — a 25 m/s shot passes that between ticks, and six of seven shots went in.

The referee's own body. The camera sits in front of the neck and moves further forward as the head tips down, or it is inside the chest and the chest (back faces) vanishes. The head is cut out of the view by the kit shader (hide_sphere, skipped in the shadow pass) rather than by shrinking the head bone, which left a neck stump in view.

The body follows the head only past 65°, and then all the way. Stopping at the edge of the dead zone left the body sideways.

Drills script their players; the AI leaves them alone. MatchAI.held — a scenario's named players run where they are sent and a given ball is kept until its cue. Left free, the passer passed before the moment and the defender on the line walked back to his position.

Every drill is checked both ways. Right answers must pass and doing nothing must fail. That caught a flag the assistant gave up on being counted as the referee waving it down.

Crowd at the big grounds is simplified. 12,000 full spectators were 32 million triangles and 10 fps on an M2; ImporterMesh.generate_lods cuts each to ~260 and the National Stadium runs at 60.

05-relations.md · 2 sections

Relations

Inside the game

  • Match owns the pitch (Venue), Ball, 22 Footballers, two Assistants, MatchAI, Laws, Assessor, Recorder and, at the National Stadium, VarSystem.
  • Referee (the player) sends every decision to Match: whistle, award, show_card, signal_advantage, accept_flag, wave_flag, allow_substitution, set_added_time.
  • Laws listens to the ball and the AI and writes Incidents; Assessor judges them at full time; ReportScreen shows the verdicts with replays from Recorder.
  • ScenarioRun puts a script on top of an ordinary match for training and challenges.
  • Game (autoload) holds Settings and Career and switches scenes.

Outside

  • Referee For Fun (~/Desktop/project/game/referee-for-fun): source of the Meshy footballer and its clip pipeline, the spectator models, fonts, UI theme, settings, controls, audio and the docs builder.
  • Sketchfab — spectator models (CC Attribution; in CREDITS.md).
  • Freesound via sfx — CC0 sound; assets/audio/SOURCES.md.
  • Local records site — docs-site gathers docs/index.html into ~/Documents/dev/docs-site/.

07-references.md · 0 sections

References

  • IFAB Laws of the Game — https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/ — the rulebook the game judges by. Used: Law 1 (field dimensions, lines belong to the areas), Law 3 (substitutions only with the ball out of play and the referee's permission), Law 5 (advantage — "at that time or within a few seconds"), Law 7 (added time), Law 8 (kick-off, change of ends), Law 9 (ball wholly over the line; ball touching a match official), Law 10 (a goal), Law 11 (offside position, involvement, no offence from a throw-in, goal kick or corner; a deliberate play by an opponent resets, a deflection or save does not), Law 12 (careless / reckless / excessive force; DOGSO and the in-area attempt-to-play-the-ball reduction; SPA and its exception where a penalty is awarded for an attempt to play the ball; advantage and cautions; back-pass; simulation), Law 14 (penalty), Law 15 (throw-in), Law 16 (goal kick), Law 17 (corner).
  • VAR protocol (IFAB) — clear and obvious error or serious missed incident; goals, penalties, direct red cards, mistaken identity; factual decisions by VAR, subjective ones by on-field review.
  • Referee For Fun's knowledge base — ~/.claude/knowledge/projects/referee-for-fun/.

2026-09-18

Luqman asked for a first-person football referee game for Windows and Mac, using Referee For Fun's resources, with walking, running and looking down at your own body, and to be asked as many questions as needed. Built

  • Ball with its own physics; posts, bar and nets; the ball can hit the referee (Law 9.2).
  • One Meshy footballer for everybody, kits repainted per player by kit.gdshader.
  • Four grounds: Riverside Rec (club linesmen, offside is yours), Station Road, Kestrel Park (fourth official), National Stadium (VAR).
  • Match AI, Laws, assistants who err on close offside, the assessor and its report.
  • First-person referee with a visible body, stamina, head bob, bumps, watch and notebook; whistle, point-to-decide, cards to the player you look at, advantage, flags, dropped ball, substitutions, added time.
  • Menus, career (3 matches a level, 8.3 average to go up), quick match, 9 drills, 8 challenges, replays from three cameras, VAR on-field reviews.
  • Football clips written in Blender; builds for macOS and Windows.

Open
