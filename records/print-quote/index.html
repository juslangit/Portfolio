Print Quote

A web page where a customer drags an STL file in and gets an instant printing price. The browser reads the triangle mesh itself, works out solid volume, shell and infill, converts that to grams and hours, and prices the job against the shop's own filament cost, machine rate and margin. Built as the portfolio piece that sits on the seam between Luqman's two skills — nobody who only does web can produce the 3D side, and nobody who only does 3D can produce the app.

Why this project exists

Research on 2026-09-10 into what makes a portfolio win freelance work said two things clearly: tutorial projects are worthless because every candidate has the same five, and clients do not care about the stack, only about a business result. An "interactive 3D portfolio" is no longer a differentiator either — it is an $85 Fiverr gig with dozens of free GitHub templates.

The differentiator is that in every one of those templates the 3D model is downloaded. Luqman makes his. He is a Blender and Unreal artist who teaches this at a vocational college and runs a BambuLab printer. A tool that needs both mesh mathematics and web engineering is one that neither side of the freelance market can copy quickly.

It also has a real buyer. Any 3D printing service that quotes by email is losing hours a week to it, and Malaysian print shops are a reachable market.

What counts as "done"

A print shop owner can put their own filament price, machine rate, setup fee and margin into the settings, hand the page's link to a customer, and get an enquiry with a price attached that they are willing to honour.

What is NOT in scope

  • Real slicing. The estimates are arithmetic on the mesh, not G-code generation.
  • Automatic support detection — supports are a checkbox the customer ticks.
  • Payment, order management, or a customer account system.
  • Formats beyond STL for v1. 3MF and OBJ are v2.

04-methods.md · 4 sections

Methods

The mathematics, so it can be defended in a client conversation

Solid volume

For every triangle, take the signed volume of the tetrahedron formed by the origin and the triangle's three vertices — dot(a, cross(b, c)) / 6. Summed over a closed mesh, the parts outside the solid cancel and what remains is the enclosed volume. This is why the answer is exact for a watertight model and nonsense for one with holes.

Surface area

Half the magnitude of the cross product of two edges, per triangle, summed.

What is actually extruded

A print is not solid.

shell = min(surface_area × walls × line_width, solid_volume) interior = solid_volume − shell infill = interior × infill_density extruded = shell + infill (+ supports if ticked)

The min matters: on a thin part the shell is the whole part, and without the clamp the estimate exceeds the solid volume.

Grams

extruded_cm³ × density. PLA 1.24, PETG 1.27, ABS 1.04, ASA 1.07, TPU 1.21 g/cm³.

Time

Volumetric flow the machine sustains:

flow = layer_height × line_width × speed × 0.42 × material_factor

The 0.42 derate covers acceleration, travel moves and cooling — a printer never holds its headline speed. Then:

seconds = extruded / flow + layers × 0.55 + 240

layers × 0.55 is the per-layer change cost; 240 s is heat-up, levelling and the purge line.

Price

material = grams / 1000 × RM per kg machine = hours × RM per hour failure = (material + machine) × failure % margin = (material + machine + failure) × margin % unit = material + machine + failure + margin total = unit × quantity + setup fee, floored at the minimum order

Gotchas worth knowing
  • STL carries no units. A file exported in inches and read as millimetres is wrong by 25.4×. Hence the units selector — this is a real thing that bites print shops, not a decorative control.
  • Binary vs ASCII STL. Do not sniff for the word solid at the start; plenty of binary files begin with it too. Check the arithmetic instead: a binary STL is exactly 84 + triangles × 50 bytes.
  • STL is Z-up, Three.js is Y-up. The mesh is rotated -PI/2 on X so the part sits on the plate correctly.
  • Orbit the camera, not the model. Rotating the group on X tilts the part through the build plate, which looks wrong to anyone who prints.
Running it

Open ~/Desktop/project/3d/print-quote/index.html in a browser. No build step, no server, no dependencies beyond the Three.js script tag.

06-decisions.md · 6 sections

Decisions

2026-09-10 — A tool on the 3D/web seam, not a 3D portfolio site

Four candidates were on the table: this calculator, a product configurator, a personal portfolio site with real 3D, and finishing the tuition platform. Research showed "interactive 3D portfolio" has become a commodity — an $85 Fiverr gig with free GitHub templates — so Three.js by itself proves nothing. What cannot be copied is that Luqman produces the 3D assets and understands FDM printing from owning a BambuLab. The calculator is the option where both are load-bearing, and it has an actual buyer in any print shop still quoting by hand.

2026-09-10 — Hand-written STL parser instead of a loader library

cdnjs carries three.js but not STLLoader, which lives in the ESM-only examples directory. Rather than risk a second CDN or inline a library, the parser was written directly — it is about forty lines for both binary and ASCII. Fewer external failure points, and the format knowledge stays in the project.

2026-09-10 — Everything runs in the browser, nothing is uploaded

No server, no file upload. Partly because it makes the result instant, but mainly because a customer's STL is their intellectual property. "The file never leaves your device" is a selling point to anyone quoting a part they have not patented yet, and it removes storage cost and liability from whoever runs the page.

2026-09-10 — Shop settings are exposed, not hard-coded

Filament RM/kg, machine RM/hour, setup fee, margin, minimum order and failure allowance are all editable on the page. A hard-coded price list makes it a demo; editable settings make it a product a shop can adopt in an afternoon, and it lets Luqman sit with a client and put their real numbers in during the conversation.

2026-09-10 — Estimates are labelled as estimates

The footer says plainly that real print time depends on geometry, orientation, supports and cooling. Overstating precision would be the fastest way to lose trust with anyone who actually prints — and a shop quotes to confirm anyway, so honest framing costs nothing and buys credibility.

2026-09-10 — Public repo under MIT

The calculator is the showcase piece, and a portfolio repository that nobody can read does not do the job a portfolio exists to do. A client evaluating Luqman should be able to open the mesh mathematics and see it is his. MIT is the right licence for something whose value is the demonstration rather than the exclusivity.

Session log

2026-09-10

What happened

Luqman asked for a website to put in his portfolio and asked me to research what would be worth building. Four searches on portfolio strategy, the Malaysian freelance web market, Three.js as a differentiator and common portfolio mistakes.

The findings that mattered: tutorial projects are worthless, clients care about business outcomes rather than the stack, the Malaysian market pays RM1,500–3,000 for WordPress sites and RM5,000–20,000 for custom web apps, and "3D portfolio" has already been commoditised into an $85 Fiverr gig. The conclusion was that the project had to sit where his 3D skills and web work overlap. He picked the STL quote calculator from four options.

Built

~/Desktop/project/3d/print-quote/index.html, published at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4473ddab-402c-415e-b363-33e7d97ccf59

  • Hand-written STL parser, binary and ASCII
  • Mesh measurement: signed-tetrahedron volume, surface area, bounding box, triangle count
  • Three.js r128 viewport with the part sitting on a to-scale Bambu build plate
  • Shell + infill model for extruded volume, density table for grams, volumetric-flow model for time
  • Printer, material, filament colour, layer height, infill, walls, quantity, units and supports as controls
  • Editable shop settings: filament RM/kg, machine RM/hour, setup, margin, minimum order, failure allowance
  • Warnings for parts that do not fit the plate, parts that only fit rotated, jobs below the minimum, TPU at fine layers, and tall parts
  • Full cost breakdown showing the working, plus a "how the number is worked out" section explaining the mathematics

Fixed before publishing

Three bugs caught on review: the canvas was opaque and would have painted black over the themed viewport background; vertical dragging rotated the model group, which tilted the part through the build plate; and the sample part was small enough that every quote hit the minimum-order floor, making the cost breakdown look inert. Camera now orbits instead of the model, canvas is alpha, sample part enlarged.

Next

  • Run real STL files through it — Luqman has models and a BambuLab, so the estimates can be checked against Bambu Studio's own slicer output
  • If the time or gram figures are off, the constants in 04-methods.md are the dials to turn
  • Consider 3MF and OBJ support, and a real enquiry form behind the order button
