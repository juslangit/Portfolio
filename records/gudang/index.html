01-idea.md · 9 sections

The idea

Problem

Every 3D asset that enters one of Luqman's games arrives from somewhere else — a Sketchfab download, a Meshy generation, occasionally a hand-built .blend — and none of it arrives usable. Three specific costs, confirmed by him on 2026-09-17:

  1. "Is this download usable?" A Sketchfab model looks right in the preview and turns out to be 400k triangles, at the wrong scale, or badly unwrapped — and he only finds out after it is already in the engine.
  2. "Which version is the good one?" Generated assets arrive as attempts. The evidence is on his own disk: referee-for-fun/assets/meshy/ holds official, official_new, athlete_meshy, player_red and player_blue — five runs at the same character, six files each, with nothing recording which one he kept or why. Also simple_character, simple_low_poly_character and random_guy: three near-identical people.
  3. "Now make it engine-ready." Naming, scale, pivots, collision and export settings are the same repetitive steps every time, and they differ between Godot and Unreal.

What this is NOT

An asset librarian. That was the first version of this idea and it was wrong. A survey of his machine on 2026-09-17 found 76 model files totalling 373 MB, almost all of it inside referee-for-fun, and only 2 .blend files anywhere. He confirmed that is essentially the whole library. Seventy-six files do not need semantic search — he can look at them. Search is a feature of this system, not its reason to exist. See D-001.

Proposed solution

Gudang ("warehouse") is the receiving bay every asset passes through on its way into a game. Drop a file in; it comes out judged, versioned and engine-ready.

  1. Inspect — headless Blender opens the file and measures it: triangle count, real-world scale, n-gons, non-manifold edges, flipped normals, UV coverage and overlap, texture resolution, material count, rig and animation presence.
  2. Judge — those measurements become a verdict per target engine (good for Godot · needs work for Unreal · not usable), each reason written in plain language with the fix, not as a raw number dump.
  3. Version — five attempts at one character become one asset with five versions, rendered side by side, one marked as the keeper with a note saying why.
  4. Prepare — fix what is mechanically fixable and export engine-ready for Godot and Unreal with the right scale, naming, pivot and collision. Reuses the pipeline and retopology add-ons he already built.
  5. Generate — when nothing in the bay fits, build it in Blender from reference. This is kilang, folded in on 2026-09-17 (D-005) as phase 2.

Why this project exists

Two jobs at once, and both are real:

  • It does actual work. direct-hit, codebot and kedai-runtuh all need assets now. Gudang has a job from its first week, which is the thing every other project in the portfolio lacks — most of them have never been used by a human.
  • It fills the hole in the portfolio. Luqman's 24 portfolio cards contain no backend, no accounts, no scheduled work, no deployment pipeline and no AI system with measured quality. The chips literally read Zero backend. Gudang needs all of those honestly, rather than bolting them on for show.

  • Built on his own expertise. He can defend every rule in the inspector from experience rather than from reading, which is the failure mode of the alternative ideas considered.
  • Turns pipeline, retopology and kilang from four separate scripts into one system.

M1 — The inspector

Goal: A script that opens any model file in headless Blender and reports what it finds, with no judgement attached yet. Done criteria:

  • done Reports triangles, real-world scale, n-gons, non-manifold edges, flipped normals, UV coverage and overlap, texture sizes, material count, rig/animation
  • done Runs over all existing files without crashing — 75/75 read, 73s, ~1s each. The "says why it could not read one" path exists but was never exercised, because nothing failed. Untested in practice, not proven
  • done Output is JSON, with the Blender version recorded alongside
  • to do Cold-start time on a GitHub Actions runner — moved to M4, where the runner actually exists. Local cost is 0.98s per file on Blender 5.2.1 LTS

Two measurements had to be corrected before they could be trusted:

  1. Blender treats an open border as non-manifold. A single-sided game asset is meant to be open, so the first run called a perfectly good chair broken in 812 places. Boundary edges and true non-manifold edges (three or more faces, or a wire edge) are now counted separately.
  2. "Recalculate outside" has no meaning on an open mesh, so inside-out faces are reported only on closed ones — null, which is not the same as zero. Open meshes get a winding-consistency check instead, which is well defined either way.

This is the reason M2 exists: numbers that look authoritative can be wrong, and the only way to know is to check them against reality.

Dependencies: Blender 4.x locally Blockers: none

M2 — Verdicts, measured

Goal: Turn measurements into a plain-language verdict per engine, and prove the verdicts are right rather than assuming it. Done criteria:

  • to do Rules produce good / needs work / not usable for Godot and for Unreal
  • to do Every reason names the fix, not just the number
  • to do A labelled corpus: 20+ assets Luqman has graded by hand
  • to do Precision and recall measured against that corpus, and the number published in the record — including if it is bad
  • to do Disagreements between the tool and Luqman are listed, not hidden

Dependencies: M1; 20 graded assets from Luqman Blockers: needs his time to grade

06-decisions.md · 5 sections

Decisions

One decision = one entry. Never delete old entries — mark them Superseded by D-00X. The point of this file: six months from now you'll ask "why did I do it this way?" — the answer is here.
D-001 — A receiving bay, not an asset librarian
  • Date: 2026-09-17
  • Status: active
  • Context: The project was proposed as an "asset librarian" — plain-language and image search over Luqman's 3D library, using local CLIP embeddings. Before writing it down, his machine was surveyed: 76 model files, 373 MB, almost all inside referee-for-fun/assets/, and only 2 .blend files in total. He confirmed that is essentially the whole library. Asked what actually costs him time, he chose three things — judging whether a download is usable, telling generated attempts apart, and making an asset engine-ready — and none of them is finding a file.
  • Options considered:
OptionProsCons
A — Librarian: semantic + image search over the libraryMultimodal retrieval is a strong AI-engineering signal76 files do not need search; solves a problem he does not have
B — Receiving bay: inspect, judge, version, prepareMatches all three pains he named; has real work to do immediatelySearch becomes a minor feature rather than the headline
  • Decision: B. Gudang is the gate every asset passes through on the way in.
  • Reason: A beautiful solution to a problem he does not have is worth nothing, and a month is too long to spend finding that out. The three pains he picked are all the same pain — judging and preparing an incoming asset — and that is a gate, not a warehouse.
  • Consequences / trade-offs: Gives up the CLIP/multimodal-retrieval story. Gains something better for interviews: an objective eval target, because a mesh either has non-manifold edges or it does not, whereas "is this search result relevant?" is a judgement call.
  • Reversible? easily — search can be added later if the library ever grows.
D-002 — Original files in Cloudflare R2, metadata in Supabase
  • Date: 2026-09-17
  • Status: active
  • Context: The project must run on free tiers only, by Luqman's explicit choice. Verified 2026-09-17: Supabase free gives 500 MB database, 1 GB file storage and 5 GB egress; Cloudflare R2 free gives 10 GB storage, 1M writes, 10M reads and no egress charges at all.
  • Decision: Original model files and their textures live in R2. Supabase holds rows, thumbnails, inspection results and embeddings.
  • Reason: His existing 373 MB would consume a third of Supabase's file storage before the project has a single user, and egress on downloads would eat the 5 GB allowance. R2's free egress makes serving files back a non-issue.
  • Consequences / trade-offs: Two storage systems to keep consistent instead of one, and R2 credentials to manage. Accepted — the alternative runs out of room almost immediately.
  • Reversible? with difficulty — moving stored files later means a migration.
D-003 — One Supabase project, one schema per app
  • Date: 2026-09-17
  • Status: active
  • Context: Supabase's free plan allows 2 active projects. Three things need a backend: gudang, auxetic-chess (its leaderboard is written and waiting on a Supabase project) and bluff-show (its TV room mode is waiting on the same thing). Three into two does not go.
  • Options considered:
OptionProsCons
A — One project per app, pay when out of slotsClean isolationCosts money, which he ruled out
B — One project, a Postgres schema per appFits the free plan; one thing to keep aliveA single blast radius; row-level security has to be right
  • Decision: B. One Supabase project with gudang, auxetic_chess and bluff_show schemas.
  • Reason: It fits the constraint he set, it is a legitimate multi-tenant design rather than a workaround, and it unblocks two stalled games as a side effect.
  • Consequences / trade-offs: Row-level security carries real weight now, which is exactly the range the project is meant to demonstrate. One paused or broken project takes down three apps.
  • Reversible? easily — a schema can be moved to its own project later.
D-004 — Headless Blender runs on GitHub Actions, not on a server
  • Date: 2026-09-17
  • Status: active
  • Context: Inspection means opening the file in Blender and measuring it, which is heavy, slow and impossible inside a Supabase edge function. A server that can run Blender costs money. Supabase free projects also auto-pause after one week with no API requests, so something has to keep the project awake regardless.
  • Decision: A job table in Postgres, and a GitHub Actions workflow that wakes on a schedule, claims pending jobs, runs Blender headless and writes results back. The same schedule keeps the Supabase project from pausing.
  • Reason: Public-repo Actions runners are free, Blender installs on them, and it turns two problems — no compute budget and the inactivity pause — into one mechanism. It is also a genuine background-work story rather than a contrived one.
  • Consequences / trade-offs: Latency is minutes, not seconds, so the UI must be built around "queued → running → done" from the start. Cold-start cost of installing Blender needs caching and has not been measured yet.
  • Reversible? easily — the worker is a script; where it runs can change.
D-005 — kilang is folded into gudang as phase 2
  • Date: 2026-09-17
  • Status: active
  • Context: kilang was opened 2026-09-13 as a separate project: an agent that builds a game-ready prop in Blender from a request. It reached planning only — no code was written and its code folder was never created. Gudang covers the same ground from the other end: kilang makes assets, gudang judges and prepares them, and both target Godot and Unreal through the same add-ons.
  • Decision: kilang becomes gudang's generator — what happens when nothing in the bay fits — and is marked archived-into-gudang.
  • Reason: Luqman's own archive note on claude-terminal says it plainly: two projects aimed at the same need is one too many. Running both would split a month of work across two half-finished systems.
  • Consequences / trade-offs: Generation is deferred behind inspection, so the prop-building idea waits. Accepted — judging assets is the pain he actually named.
  • Reversible? easily — kilang's notes are kept, not deleted.

Judgement is M2, and M2 is the milestone that decides whether the project is worth the month, because it is where the verdicts get measured against Luqman's own grading rather than assumed correct.

Not on the portfolio yet: nothing to show, and every claim there has to trace to an artifact.

Built

src/inspect_blend.py runs inside headless Blender and writes down what is in one model file as JSON. src/inspect.py is the outside half: it finds Blender, gives each file its own process with a time limit, and collects the reports. Repo initialised at ~/Desktop/project/3d/gudang with a .gitignore covering .env, and a README.

Measuring is kept strictly apart from judging. Whether 180,000 triangles is too many depends on the engine and on what the asset is for; that is M2's job, and keeping it in a different file means the rules can be changed without re-opening a single model.

Two numbers that were wrong

The first run on a plastic chair reported 812 non-manifold edges and 512 flipped faces out of 1,048 triangles. Both were artefacts, caught only because the numbers were implausible for a chair:

  1. edge.is_manifold is False for an open border, and a single-sided game asset — a chair shell, a curtain, a signboard — is meant to be open. Boundary edges and true non-manifold edges are now counted separately.
  2. recalc_face_normals has no meaning on an open mesh, because there is no "outside" to be flipped away from. Inside-out faces are now reported only on closed meshes, as null otherwise — which is not the same as zero. Open meshes get a winding-consistency check, which is well defined either way.

The lesson is the argument for M2 in miniature: a measurement that looks authoritative can be nonsense, and assuming otherwise would have poisoned every verdict built on top.

What it found in 75 real files

Read 75/75 in 73 seconds, about a second each, on Blender 5.2.1 LTS.

  • Scale is a Sketchfab problem, not a Meshy problem. 16 of 32 Sketchfab downloads are at an implausible scale; 0 of 33 Meshy files are. A WW2 fighter plane 1,757 m long, a submarine at 1,476 m, a plastic chair 76 m tall sitting 250 m from its own origin. That is a rule M2 can act on immediately.
  • The version problem is worse than described. Six files share player_blue's exact triangle count, six more share player_red's, five share official's, five share athlete_meshy's, four share official_new's.
  • A finding for referee-for-fun: athlete_average, athlete_stocky, athlete_tall, athlete_wiry and spectator all have an identical 2,816 triangles, so the four "body types" are very probably one mesh at different scales. Strong evidence, not proof — confirming it means comparing vertex positions, which gudang cannot do yet.
  • 74 of 75 models are open, only 1 watertight; 35 carry an armature; 157 textures, 12 of them not power-of-two; 3 models have no UVs at all.
